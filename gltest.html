Known, unavoidable differences

1. 'lighten' with source alpha below 1. Canvas2D computes dst + a·max(0, src − dst); GL's MAX blend computes max(dst, a·src). They agree exactly where the instance is opaque (a = 1), which is how the suite tests it; the "lighten, alpha < 1" row measures the gap. Keep 'lighten' layers opaque, or move them to 'lighter' / 'screen', which are exact.

2. 8-bit rounding. GL quantises each instance's premultiplied colour to 8 bits before blending; GPU-raster Canvas2D (Skia) does the same on its batched path (an alpha sweep matched 64/64) but not on every op, so a translucent blend can differ by 1. Software-raster Canvas2D (what Chrome falls back to on some devices, and what a canvas becomes after repeated getImageData) rounds differently again: the CONTROL rows compare Canvas2D with itself, GPU against software, and show a spread as large as GL's. The gate (against GPU Canvas2D): geometry exact (no texel, row, dash or clip off: that would be a large delta), max delta ≤ 4, under 0.5% of pixels off by more than 1. Columns are laid as two instances (fringe, then core) so GL blends exactly as often as Canvas2D does; opts.fuseColumns folds them into one quad (half the instances, one more rounding step).

3. Sprite tint. The GL sprite material can tint; Canvas2D cannot without a tinted copy, so the suite draws sprites untinted.

4. 'overlay' (Akihabara's rain sheets) has no fixed-function blend: it needs the destination in the shader. It stays in Canvas2D, or the rain sheets move to 'screen' / 'lighter' when they move to GL.

5. A mixed GL layer (additive pools and source-over dark breaks in one transparent GL canvas, composited source-over) is close but not exact: the layer stores its colour in 8 bits once more (deltas of 2-3), and light that has already clipped at white over the road before a dark break clips in the one-canvas picture but not in the layer (a few pixels, up to ~20). An additive-only layer composited 'lighter' (CSS mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter) is exact. See the design note's layering plan.
